02 · Hugoniot

Peak shock pressure

Planar impact approximation with a linear shock–particle Hugoniot, Us = C0 + S·up, solved by impedance match so the impactor and target need not be the same material. The pressure is then mapped onto the Stöffler shock stages.

Caveats

What these will and won't tell you

Every one of these is a first-order estimate. Treat them as a way to check whether a number is plausible, not as a substitute for a model.

Vein cooling

Assumes constant thermal properties, no latent heat of crystallisation, and a host that is infinite and initially uniform. Latent heat holds the vein hotter for longer than this curve shows, so quench times here are lower bounds.

Shock pressure

The planar impact approximation holds only near the impact point, in the first moments. It ignores porosity, which raises shock heating sharply, and pre-shock temperature. Real peak pressures decay steeply away from the contact.

Crater scaling

Gravity-regime scaling for competent rock. It is wrong in the strength regime (small craters, strong targets), wrong for very oblique impacts below about 15°, and does not model atmospheric break-up of the impactor.
